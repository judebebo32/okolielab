The projects

Two ways to make critical materials circular

Both projects combine experiments with data science and process modelling, both lead to autonomous experimental platforms, and both build directly on the group's work on spent-battery upcycling, adsorbent materials and AI-guided design.

01Project 1 · MSc or PhD

Development of an autonomous experiment for critical mineral recovery from spent lithium batteries

Spent lithium-ion batteries are a growing source of lithium, nickel, cobalt and manganese. Finding the leaching and separation conditions that recover these metals cleanly takes hundreds of experiments. This project will build a self-driving laboratory that runs, measures and plans those experiments on its own.

What you will do

  • Design and assemble an automated leaching and separation platform that combines liquid handling, temperature-controlled mixing and in-line measurements.
  • Use machine learning and Bayesian optimization so the platform chooses its next experiment from the results it has already measured.
  • Screen greener lixiviants, including deep eutectic solvents, for selective recovery of Li, Ni, Co and Mn from battery black mass.
  • Link the best conditions to process models, techno-economic analysis and life-cycle assessment.

02Project 2 · MSc or PhD

A continuous system using functionalized porous materials for rare earth metal recovery from heterogeneous streams

Rare earth elements are present at low concentrations in complex streams such as industrial and mining wastewaters and electronic-waste leachates, alongside many competing ions and solids. This project will develop functionalized porous adsorbents and a continuous-flow system that captures rare earths selectively and can be regenerated and reused. As its final deliverable, the project will turn this system into an autonomous experiment that runs, measures and optimizes the separation on its own.

What you will do

  • Synthesize and functionalize porous materials (e.g., biomass-derived carbons, biochar and metal–organic frameworks) with groups that bind rare earth ions selectively.
  • Characterize the materials by surface area and porosity analysis, spectroscopy and microscopy.
  • Run batch and continuous fixed-bed tests on realistic multi-component streams, measuring breakthrough, selectivity, elution and regeneration.
  • Build transport and process models to scale up the column design and assess cost and environmental impact.
  • Final deliverable: automate the continuous system into an autonomous experiment, with in-line sensing and machine-learning-guided optimization of adsorption and regeneration.

Equity, diversity & inclusion

Our commitment to diversity

The SETD Research Group selects students on the basis of merit and is firmly committed to equity, diversity and inclusion. We believe that people with different backgrounds, experiences and ways of thinking make our research stronger, and we work to make sure every member of the group feels welcome, respected and able to do their best work.

We welcome applications from women, Indigenous persons, racialized persons and members of visible minorities, persons with disabilities, ethnic and religious minorities, persons of minority sexual orientations and gender identities, first-generation university students and international applicants, as well as from all qualified candidates with the skills and knowledge to engage productively with diverse communities.

Applicants who need an accommodation at any stage of the application process because of a disability or for another reason are invited to contact Dr. Okolie at jude.okolie@bucknell.edu. Requests are handled confidentially.
